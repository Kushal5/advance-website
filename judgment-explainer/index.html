Fiji Judgment Explainer

Court judgments are public. Understanding them shouldn't need a law degree. Give a case number and get a plain-English explanation of a published Fiji judgment, with every point showing the paragraph it comes from.

How to use it

  1. Type a case number in the chat, for example HBC 131 of 2023 or ABU 6 of 2023.
  2. Confirm it's the right case.
  3. You get a plain-English summary: who was involved, what happened, what the court decided and what it ordered.

How it works

  • Official source. It looks the judgment up on the Fiji Judiciary's public website, not a general web search.
  • Reads the whole thing. It reads every page of the judgment before explaining it, including scanned ones.
  • You can check it. Every point shows the paragraph number it comes from, like [¶12], and the court's orders are quoted word for word.

Good to know

  • It only covers published judgments. If a case isn't on the Judiciary website, it will tell you rather than guess.
  • It explains one judgment at a time and only answers questions about Fiji judgments.
  • Names of victims and children stay anonymised, as they are in the judgment.
  • This is a beta. It may make mistakes, so check the paragraph references against the judgment.

General information only, not legal advice. For your own situation, speak to a lawyer or the Legal Aid Commission.
